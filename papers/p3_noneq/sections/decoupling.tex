\section{Response asymmetry and dissipation, measured apart on strategic games}
\label{sec:decoupling}

Both $\R$ and $\EPR$ vanish exactly on potential games and read loudly on
harmonic ones, so a natural conjecture (C1 in the programme's ledger) held
that the four meters co-move tightly along the whole family. Marginally they
do: over 1{,}000 games ($100 \times 10$ $\alpha$ levels, $\lambda = 1.2$,
\emph{two players}, $3\times3$), Spearman $\rho(\EPR, \R) = 0.993$.
\textbf{Scope fence, up front rather than at the end:} everything in this
section is measured at $N = 2$, and the low-$\alpha$ co-movement it starts from
is now known to be a two-player fact --- at matched effective precision
$\rho_S(\EPR,\R)$ at $\alpha = 0.05$ falls $+0.85$ ($N{=}2$) $\to +0.38$
($N{=}3$) $\to +0.17$ ($N{=}4$), so at $N \ge 3$ there is nothing to collapse
\emph{from} (F-0023).

\begin{finding}[Conditional decoupling and sign reversal; F-0004]
The marginal agreement is $\alpha$-confounding. Conditional on the level,
$\rho(\EPR, \R)$ is $+0.80..+0.88$ for $\alpha \le 0.65$, degrades through
$+0.61$ ($\alpha=0.75$) and $+0.32$ ($\alpha=0.85$), and \emph{reverses} to
$-0.355$ at $\alpha = 0.95$: among near-pure-harmonic games, larger response
asymmetry associates with \emph{less} dissipation
(Figure~\ref{fig:decoupling}). This finding originated as a red-team
objection to our own headline correlation; the stratified metric is now a
permanent artifact field. \textbf{The reversal is quoted only with its
$(m, \lambda, N)$ scope}: $m = 3$, $\lambda = 1.2$, $N = 2$. It is
$\lambda$-dependent (F-0010), it survives to $m = 6$ only once the normalised
precision is held fixed by construction (F-0024: $-0.206$ $[-0.300,-0.105]$ at
$m{=}6$ and matched $\bar\lambda$, against $-0.112$ $[-0.214,-0.016]$ at fixed
Frobenius scale), and it has no $N > 2$ analogue because the baseline coupling
it is a departure from does not exist there. What is robust, and what the
programme's flagship claim rests on, is the \emph{decorrelation}, not the
sign.
\end{finding}

\paragraph{Mechanism, by preregistered test (F-0007).} We proposed a repair:
perhaps only $\R$'s \emph{denominator} $\lVert\chieq + \chieq{}^\top\rVert$
misbehaves at high $\alpha$ (H2), while the antisymmetric numerator keeps
tracking dissipation (H1) --- in which case a numerator-only meter would
restore coupling. On the identical seeded families, paired by game:
\textbf{H2 confirmed} --- at $\alpha=0.95$,
$\rho(\R,\, 1/\lVert\chieq{+}\chieq{}^\top\rVert) = 0.993$, rising
monotonically from $\approx 0$ at low $\alpha$; $\R$'s within-level variation
in the near-harmonic regime is entirely the shrinking symmetric response.
\textbf{H1 refuted} --- the numerator \emph{also} decouples:
$\rho(\lVert\chieq{-}\chieq{}^\top\rVert, \EPR) = -0.37$ at $\alpha = 0.95$,
down from $+0.77..0.86$ at $\alpha \le 0.65$. No renormalisation of the
response matrix recovers the dissipation ordering.

\paragraph{The structural reading, and whose it is.} This negative result is the
better result. $\chieq$ is a \emph{local derivative} of the equilibrium map at
the QRE point; $\EPR$ is a \emph{global functional of the stationary flux} of
the joint-profile chain. They co-vary exactly as long as a potential component
modulates both, and part company when it vanishes. \textbf{That a local response
object and a global dissipation object can be structurally inequivalent is not
new, and is not claimed here as new.} For Markov jump processes --- the class our
Glauber generator belongs to --- \citet{baiesi2009} show that the non-equilibrium
response splits into a time-antisymmetric (entropic) and a time-symmetric
(frenetic) term, so response is not a functional of entropy production alone; and
the statement is class-dependent rather than universal, since for Langevin systems
\citet{haradasasa2005} prove an exact \emph{equality} between the integrated
fluctuation--response violation and the dissipation rate. That equality does not
reach the objects used here: $\R$ is the reciprocity defect of a \emph{static}
equilibrium susceptibility rather than a time-integrated response--correlation
difference, and $\EPR$ is a cycle functional on a discrete chain with no velocity
variable to integrate. What this section contributes is the instantiation on
strategic games, the $\alpha$ coordinate along which the breakdown is traced, and
the measurement of where along it the two meters stop being substitutes. Consequently the
instrument-scope table of \citep{p1} is not a convenience but a necessity:
$\R$ answers ``is this system potential?'' (exactly, at every $\lambda$);
$\EPR$ answers ``how hard is it driven?''; and in the near-harmonic regime
neither substitutes for the other. Conjecture C1 is thereby resolved as:
co-movement for $\alpha \lesssim 0.65$, structural failure above. The
crossover's $\lambda$- and size-dependence (open in v0.1) is now measured
(F-0010): the \emph{collapse} of within-level coupling at $\alpha = 0.95$
is universal --- from $\approx 0.90$ at $\alpha \le 0.65$ to $\le 0.25$
in every condition tested ($\lambda \in \{0.8, 1.2, 2.0\}$, both $3\times
3$ and $4\times 4$) --- while the \emph{negative sign} is a second,
$\lambda$-dependent effect ($+0.25 \to +0.03 \to -0.23$ across the three
$\lambda$; $-0.26$ at $4\times 4$), with each individual sign within
$\sim$2 null-SD at $n = 60$. We record the methodological event plainly:
the initial criterion (reversal in $\ge 3/4$ conditions) \emph{failed} and
the failure is the finding --- the robust fact is decorrelation, not
anti-alignment. The obvious follow-on explanation, that the sign tracks depth
into the supercritical wedge, was tested and \textbf{refuted}: $0$ of $180$
games at these $(\alpha,\lambda)$ are supercritical at all, so the whole
effect lives outside the wedge (F-0010 addendum). The residual candidate is
$\lambda$-dependent equilibrium concentration, and it is a hypothesis, not a
result.
