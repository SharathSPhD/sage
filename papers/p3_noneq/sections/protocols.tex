\section{Driving the system: quench thermodynamics}
\label{sec:protocols}

The readings so far are stationary. Real strategic environments are
\emph{driven}: incentive sharpness moves — information arrives, stakes
change, $\lambda$ ramps. Unit \texttt{thermo.protocols} instantiates the
Hatano--Sasa decomposition \citep{hatanosasa2001, espositovdb2010} exactly on
the Glauber chain: for any distribution $p$ evolving under the generator at
the current $\lambda$,
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}(p) \;=\; \underbrace{\sigma_{\mathrm{hk}}(p)}_{\text{holds the NESS}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\sigma_{\mathrm{ex}}(p)}_{-\,\tfrac{d}{dt} D(p\,\|\,\pi)},
\]
both parts nonnegative, with $\sigma_{\mathrm{hk}} \equiv 0$ precisely under
detailed balance — on potential games, dissipation is pure relaxation. For
stepwise $\lambda$-protocols the Hatano--Sasa integral fluctuation theorem
$\langle e^{-Y}\rangle = 1$ holds for \emph{every} game, including off
detailed balance where Jarzynski does not apply. One honesty note our
red-team insisted on and we verified: for stepwise protocols the
weighted-transfer computation of $\langle e^{-Y}\rangle$ telescopes to $1$
algebraically, so it is an identity verification, not a bug-catcher; the
correctness test is the independent sampled-path check, powered at
$\alpha = 0.5$ where $\langle Y\rangle \sim 10^{-2}$ (CI $[0.995, 1.003]
\ni 1$; artifacts \texttt{protocol\_ift\_checks.json}).

\paragraph{The driving-cost inversion (F-0012).} A pre-registered scan
(predictions written in config before the run; the intended prior commit
aborted on a hook failure and the audit gap is recorded, not smoothed over)
ramped $\lambda: 0.5 \to 3$ across the $\alpha$ family. Excess dissipation
obeys the discrete quasi-static law $\langle Y\rangle \propto 1/K$ in the
number of steps (measured slopes $-1.02$, $-0.94$) and \emph{collapses}
three orders of magnitude with $\alpha$ ($3.6\times10^{-2} \to
2.8\times10^{-5}$ nats), while accumulated housekeeping grows to $12.4$ nats,
exactly linear in protocol duration ($\sigma_{\mathrm{hk}}$ depends only on
the generator and its $\pi$, so per-hold constancy is structural; the
informative number is the burn \emph{rate}, $0.77$ nats per unit time at
$\alpha = 0.95$). The cost of driving inverts across the family: potential
games pay per \emph{change} and drive for free in the fine-step limit;
near-harmonic games pay \emph{rent per unit time} whether driven or not.

\paragraph{The mechanism, found by the machine (F-0013).} Why does excess
collapse? Four quantitative hypotheses — a payoff-scale fold, the
NESS-sensitivity floor $F = \sum_k D(\pi_{k-1}\|\pi_k)$, a spectral-gap lag
model, and a quadratic strawman — were handed to an expected-free-energy
experiment selector (unit \texttt{estimate.bayes}): each hypothesis predicts
every candidate probe's outcome, the next probe maximises the BALD mutual
information between hypothesis and outcome, and beliefs update by Bayes. The
campaign chose the single most discriminating probe available
($\alpha = 0.95$, $1.19$ nats of expected information) and resolved in one
round: the \emph{floor} hypothesis at posterior $0.996$, then validated on
all 19 unconsumed probes (median residual $0.002$ dex). Scope, stated
plainly: in the well-relaxed regime the floor formula is exact-in-the-limit
($\langle Y\rangle \to F$), so the campaign's content there is regime
identification plus generalisation (the $3{\times}3$ family agrees to
$0.1\%$); at fast quenches lag dominates and the floor formula fails by design.
A second campaign (F-0014) closed that remainder at first order — with its
own instructive failure first: the selector stopped confidently after one
probe and the pre-registered held-out guard \emph{refused} the verdict
(\texttt{winner\_failed\_validation}), a lesson now institutionalised as
the loop's \texttt{min\_probes} stopping gate. The validated picture:
excess is bracketed by the path-length floor ($\tau \to \infty$) and the
frozen divergence $D(\pi_{\mathrm{start}}\|\pi_{\mathrm{end}})$ — exact
\emph{at} $\tau = 0$ but with a non-uniform approach — and a single
spectral-gap crossover $\langle Y\rangle \approx F + (D_{\mathrm{frozen}}
- F)e^{-g\tau}$ holds to $\sim 0.1$ dex on monotone NESS paths (held-out
median $0.089$). Where the NESS path is loop-like ($\pi_{\mathrm{end}}
\approx \pi_{\mathrm{start}}$ with excursions between, as at
$\alpha = 0.95$ with a long ramp) the formula fails by $\sim 1$ dex, and
that failure is reported, not averaged away. A third campaign (F-0015)
then replaced the global formula with a \emph{per-step, path-aware}
recursion — $p_k = \pi_k + (p_{k-1} - \pi_k)e^{-g_k\tau}$ with $g_k$ the
gap at the current $\lambda_k$, $Y$ accumulated along the tracked
distribution — which dominates on both median ($0.076$ vs $0.13$ dex,
$3{\times}3$ games included) and worst case ($0.44$ vs $1.11$: the
loop-path failure is structural to endpoint comparison and vanishes when
the path is followed). Two process notes belong in print: the campaign's
first two-mode ``result'' was retracted as an implementation artifact
caught by adversarial review, and the corrected two-mode model
\emph{still} loses to one mode — truncating the fast remainder at each
switch errs more than damping the whole deviation at the gap rate.

\paragraph{Quench dissipation from data (F-0016).} The identities above
use the generator; data provides only state windows. The plug-in estimator
(unit \texttt{thermo.hs\_estimator}) pools each hold's occupation into
$\hat\pi_k$ and accumulates $\hat Y$ at the switches --- and its
certification arc is the programme's discipline in miniature: a first
adversarial review was \emph{withheld} (the natural self-check, the IFT
$\langle e^{-\hat Y}\rangle = 1$, proved structurally insufficient --- a
45\% bias can hide behind an IFT of $1.01$); the chase refuted two
statistical suspects and then found a real defect (a missing pre-quench
window silently dropping one jump term); a 20-seed study showed the
corrected estimator unbiased with calibrated intervals (20/20 at nominal
95\%); and the working gate is \emph{physical}, not statistical --- every
hold must exceed four times its own autocorrelation-estimated relaxation
time, with the IFT retained as a tested anomaly detector that does fire on
genuinely non-stepwise input. One consequence deserves emphasis: because
the spectral gap collapses as $\lambda$ concentrates
(Section~\ref{sec:phase}), the holds a data-side reading requires grow
precisely where stakes sharpen --- quench dissipation is hardest to
measure exactly where strategic systems are most interesting.

\paragraph{Two failure modes that looked like one (F-0019, F-0020).}
Applying the estimator to a month of market data ($n \approx 30$
trajectories, against a certified floor of $n \ge 200$) fails, and the
reason turned out to be two distinct defects that a single statistic had
conflated. Interval \emph{coverage} survives all the way down to $n = 20$
(19--20 of 20 seeds, at width $\approx 1.9\times$ the bootstrap standard
error), so the \emph{number} is sound on a month of data; what fails is the
instrument's own \emph{verdict}. Decomposing that failure required a
permutation argument. The order of the trajectories is not a physical
property, so permuting it must leave every verdict unchanged; it did not,
and a permutation restricted to preserve the composition of the $i{:}{:}4$
trajectory split used to estimate the relaxation time's standard error made
the instability largely vanish --- localising it in one implementation
choice rather than in the physics.

Replacing that split with order-invariant estimators (a leave-one-out
jackknife computable in $O(nT)$ from the autocorrelation's sufficient
statistics, an analytic delta-method propagation, and a trajectory
bootstrap) confirms the diagnosis and refutes the extrapolation drawn from
it. Flag flips fall to \emph{zero} at every $n$, and on real
day-ahead price data the flip rate under physically-null day-order
shuffles falls from $0.214$ to $0.050$, with one month collapsing from
$17/20$ to $0/20$; the choice of standard error even moves an admit/refuse
decision on a real month while leaving the estimate identical to six
decimals. Yet agreement with the exact settling status at $n = 30$ still
fails for every candidate. The reason is instructive: measured against an
oracle standard error from independent replicates, the trajectory bootstrap
is the most \emph{accurate} of the four ($0.18$--$0.29$ relative deviation,
against $0.44$--$0.49$ for the incumbent), and the relaxation-time estimate
itself carries an across-seed standard deviation of $35$--$40\%$ at
$n = 30$. An accurate error bar must \emph{report} that variance, and must
therefore refuse holds that are genuinely settled. Flag stability was
implementation-bound and is solved; verdict accuracy at small $n$ is a
property of the relaxation-time estimator, and the remaining lever is a
lower-variance $\hat\tau$ rather than a better error bar. The same
permutation argument then exposed a second, independent order-dependence in
the interval bootstrap itself --- and there the obstruction is structural:
a hard Boolean thresholded on a Monte-Carlo interval whose bound sits at
the threshold cannot be made stable by any choice of seed, only by
reporting the margin or driving the noise well below it.
