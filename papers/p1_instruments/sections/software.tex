\texttt{strataq} is a uv-workspace monorepo (\texttt{github.com/SharathSPhD/sage})
in JAX with float64 enforced at import --- the susceptibility and dissipation
computations are ill-conditioned near criticality and float32 is not
acceptable there. Architecture in brief: three mathematical \emph{engines}
(finite strategic form; population games; Bayesian, deferred by recorded
decision), with solvers, implicit differentiation and the dynamics/entropy
machinery shared in an engine-agnostic core. Domains --- congestion, Blotto,
pricing, electricity --- are \emph{plugins}: exactly five declared objects
(payoff oracle, action-grid builder, conjugate-field spec, loader, learn page)
and zero core changes, enforced by a commit hook and CI. The conjugate-field
declaration is load-bearing: a domain that cannot name an observable payoff
perturbation (e.g.\ penalty kicks) declares \texttt{NONE}, and the response
instruments \emph{refuse} to run for it rather than returning a meaningless
number.

Reproducibility is contractual, not aspirational. A work unit closes only when
its \emph{gate} --- code (tests, strict types, lint, coverage, no stubs),
domain (the claim demonstrably true, with machine-readable artifacts),
statistical (effect sizes with intervals, justified $n$, recorded seeds),
documentation, and adversarial (a red-team review that sees the artifact and
claim but never the implementation rationale) --- is green, and every domain
artifact must regenerate from its recorded seed via \texttt{make reproduce}.
Anti-gaming rules are mechanical: deleting or \texttt{xfail}-ing a failing test
blocks the commit; weakening a domain gate requires a recorded architecture
decision; gate regressions fail CI. The adversarial layer earned its keep: of
the roughly twenty objections raised across the units behind this paper, three
were blocking defects in our own enforcement tooling, one exposed the
marginal--conditional trap of Finding~1, and one forced the epistemic
disclosures around Finding~3.

Performance notes for users: solvers are JIT-compiled (the 2{,}000-game
$\alpha$ sweep with dissipation readings completes in $\sim$20\,s on CPU);
the separable Hodge transform is near-linear in tensor size; the dense
dynamics path is exact and intended for small games, with trajectory
estimators (KLD, TUR bounds) planned for scale. \texttt{pygambit} is an
optional oracle dependency, never a runtime requirement.
