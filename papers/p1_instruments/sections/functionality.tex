A map of the public surface as it stands. Notation: players $i$ with finite
action sets, mixed profiles $\sigma_i$, precision $\lambda_i$;
$C_i=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i)-\sigma_i\sigma_i^\top$;
$S=\operatorname{blockdiag}(\lambda_iC_i)$;
$B_{ij}(a,b)=\partial U_i(a)/\partial\sigma_j(b)$.

\paragraph{Solve.} \texttt{logit\_qre} is a JIT-compiled damped
Krasnoselskii--Mann iteration on the logit fixed point. \texttt{mirror} adds
magnetic mirror descent \citep{sokota2023} --- a structurally different,
last-iterate-convergent method --- so that solver dependence can be \emph{tested}
rather than assumed away. \texttt{homotopy} is a pseudo-arclength branch tracer
with fold detection by $\lambda$-direction reversal and $\rho(SB)$ recorded along
the branch, so a turning point of the correspondence is a labelled event rather
than a silent jump. \texttt{implicit} differentiates through the fixed point with
a custom VJP on the transposed tangent system, sharing the $(I-SB)$
factorisation.

\paragraph{Respond and decompose.} \texttt{chi\_partial} is the opponents-frozen
identity $\chipart=\lambda_iC_i$; \texttt{chi\_equilibrium} is
$\chieq=(I-SB)^{-1}S$; \texttt{reciprocity\_defect} is
$\R=\lVert\chieq-\chieq{}^\top\rVert_F/\lVert\chieq+\chieq{}^\top\rVert_F$, zero
if and only if the normalised game is potential. Two properties are documented at
the call site because both have been misread in print, including by us: $\R$ is a
ratio of norms rather than a fraction, so it is unbounded above (matching pennies
reads $1.20$), and only its \emph{zero} is $\lambda$-free --- the magnitude
scales with $\lambda$, so any level carries the $\lambda$ it was read at.
\texttt{strategic\_spectrum} and \texttt{critical\_lambda} locate criticality
from the same operator, warning rather than returning a huge $\chieq$ near it.
\texttt{hodge\_decompose} and \texttt{alpha} give the geometry, and
\texttt{make\_family} constructs games at an exact target $\alpha$, which turns
an $\alpha$-sweep into a controlled experiment.

\paragraph{Dynamics and thermodynamics.} \texttt{core.dynamics} builds the
Glauber generator on the joint profile space with its stationary distribution,
currents $J^*$, exact $\EPR$ and a uniformised trajectory sampler.
\texttt{thermo.estimators} holds the data-side estimators;
\texttt{thermo.nulls} the surrogate classes, including a reversibilized-Markov
null matching an observed chain's persistence, which is the sharp test for
pair-level detailed balance; \texttt{thermo.protocols} the Hatano--Sasa
\citep{hatanosasa2001} housekeeping/excess split and stepwise $\lambda$-quench
protocols with their fluctuation theorems; and \texttt{thermo.hs\_estimator} the
plug-in quench estimator with a standalone \texttt{relaxation\_gate} that refuses
windows which have not settled.

\paragraph{Estimate.} \texttt{estimate.lam} runs four structurally different
$\lambda$ estimators --- frequency MLE with a profile-likelihood interval,
autodiff MLE through an unrolled solve, $\chi$ moment-matching, dispersion
inversion --- with an agreement protocol treating their disagreement as a
misspecification diagnostic. \texttt{estimate.bayes} adds a grid posterior with a
self-diagnosing resolution guard, mixture Bayes factors and an
expected-free-energy experiment-selection loop. \texttt{fit()} packages the
workflow of Section~1 for tidy experimental data.

\paragraph{Repeated, evolutionary, extensive.} \texttt{repeated} writes
strategies as Moore machines, so grim trigger and tit-for-tat are data rather
than code paths; it answers the folk theorem's two questions (sustainable at
$\delta$? critical $\delta$?) in closed form for grim trigger and generically by
one-shot deviation, adds a logit analogue in which sustainability is a
probability, and measures Edgeworth cycles \citep{maskin1988} from alternating
logit best response on a price ladder. \texttt{evolutionary} has replicator
dynamics in continuous and discrete time, rest points by support enumeration
classified by tangent-space eigenvalues, the logit dynamic whose rest points are
the logit QRE, and the finite-population Moran side; the two meet at one scalar,
because the selection intensity $\beta$ of the Fermi rule \emph{is} the logit
precision $\lambda$, and \texttt{compare\_intensity} reports both readings with
the gap between them. \texttt{extensive} stores trees as flat preorder arrays
with a nested-dict constructor that doubles as the JSON format, and implements
the two tree passes, the reduced normal form, Kuhn's theorem \citep{kuhn1953},
backward induction, a catalogue of classics, and the agent QRE
\citep{mckelvey1998} --- logit at every information set --- on the same damped
fixed point as the strategic-form solver, cross-validated against
\texttt{pygambit} (Section~6).

\paragraph{Problems, verdicts, figures.} \texttt{strataq.problems} exposes eight
problem types --- pricing, auction, routing, allocation, electricity, repeated,
evolutionary, extensive --- on one contract: construct, solve, summarise.
Problems validate on construction with actionable errors; solutions are frozen
dataclasses whose \texttt{.diagnostics} carry the response, decomposition and
dissipation readings, computed only if asked for. \texttt{solve\_situation()}
assembles a whole recommendation once, including a sensitivity block that
re-solves across a ladder of precisions and reports what fraction of it agrees
and where the answer first changes --- a recommendation holding from $\lambda/4$
to $4\lambda$ is a different object from one holding only at the $\lambda$ you
picked, and the API says which one you have. \texttt{diagnose()} is the one-call
entry point: a verdict, with \texttt{explain()} for every warning, band, null and
seed, \texttt{snippet()} for a runnable reproduction of exactly that reading, and
\texttt{plot()} for the point in the $(\R,\EPR)$ plane. \texttt{strataq.viz}
carries one palette shared by library, application and papers.
