\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wherever a population of agents responds to incentives imperfectly, the same mathematical
object appears: a softmax over payoffs. Logit quantal response equilibrium (QRE) makes that
object an equilibrium concept---each agent's mixed strategy is an entropy-regularised best
response to everyone else's~\cite{mckelvey1995,goeree2016}---and the regularisation weight
$\lambda$ plays the role of an inverse temperature. The correspondence with statistical
mechanics is not decorative. In an exact potential game~\cite{monderer1996}, logit revision
dynamics are reversible with stationary distribution $\pi^*\propto e^{\lambda\Phi}$, the
probability current vanishes identically, and the entropy production is
zero~\cite{blume1993}. The system \emph{is} a Gibbs equilibrium, not merely like one.

A \emph{strategic non-equilibrium steady state} is what you get when that fails. Generic
games are not potential; their revision dynamics break detailed balance and settle into a
stationary state with circulating probability current and strictly positive entropy
production~\cite{schnakenberg1976,seifert2012}. The question this paper is about is how one
should \emph{measure} that, given only observed behaviour.

Two measurements are natural, and both are already in use. The first is local. Perturb one
agent's payoffs and observe how every agent's equilibrium mixture moves; the resulting
matrix $\chieq=(I-SB)^{-1}S$ is the strategic analogue of a susceptibility, and its
asymmetry is a direct test of Onsager reciprocity. The second is global. Follow the joint
profile as it wanders, and measure how much the forward trajectory statistics differ from
the reverse; that is the Schnakenberg entropy production of the revision
chain~\cite{schnakenberg1976}. Both vanish exactly when the normalised game is potential.
Both are estimable from data without knowing payoffs---the first from cross-agent
pass-through asymmetry, the second from trajectory irreversibility. It is therefore natural,
and nearly universal in practice, to treat them as two views of one underlying quantity:
\emph{the} distance from equilibrium.

Prior work has not tested that assumption, because the tools to test it did not coexist. The
harmonic fraction $\alpha$ of the Candogan flow decomposition~\cite{candogan2011} gives a
basis-independent measure of how far a game is from potential, but fifteen years after its
introduction there is no published software implementing it. Entropy-production estimators
for observed trajectories are mature~\cite{otsubo2022,seifert2026}, but they operate on
scalar time series and have no representation of agents, actions or profiles. QRE solvers
are excellent~\cite{turocy2005,gambit} but expose no derivative of the equilibrium. Testing
whether the two measurements agree requires all three at once, on the same game, at
controlled $\alpha$.

\paragraph{Scope, stated before the claim.} This paper is not a new equilibrium concept,
not a new solver, and not a claim that either meter is incorrect. Both are computed here
exactly as their originating literatures define them; the QRE correspondence itself is
validated against the reference implementation~\cite{gambit} to $10^{-8}$. The contribution
is a statement about the \emph{dimension} of the space these meters live in, and an
instrument for locating a system inside it.

\paragraph{The dictionary.} In what follows, the ``state'' is a joint action profile of a
finite game; the ``energy'' is the negative expected payoff and the ``potential'' is
Monderer--Shapley's $\Phi$ where it exists; the ``inverse temperature'' is the logit
precision $\lambda$; the ``susceptibility'' is the derivative of equilibrium play with
respect to a payoff field; the ``current'' is the net probability flux between profiles
under logit revision; and ``distance from equilibrium'' is precisely the thing this paper
argues is not one number.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{(C1)}] \textbf{The two-axis result.} Response asymmetry $\R$ and dissipation
$\EPR$ share a zero and nothing else. Unconditionally they co-move at $\rho_S=0.993$; once
the harmonic fraction is conditioned on, the co-movement degrades monotonically and
\emph{decorrelates}---from $\rho_S=+0.72$ at $\alpha=0.05$ to $+0.01$ $[-0.14,+0.16]$ at
$\alpha=0.95$ at the largest action count tested, intervals disjoint, against that same
$0.993$ marginal. Decorrelation, not anti-correlation, is what independence requires and
what is claimed here; the negative residual seen at $m\le5$ is reported with its
$(m,\lambda)$ scope attached and is not claimed generally. The unconditional agreement is a
confound: both meters are reading $\alpha$ ($\rho_S=0.990$ and $0.982$ respectively).
\item[\textbf{(C2)}] \textbf{A refuted repair, pre-registered.} The natural explanation---
that $\R$ is a norm ratio whose denominator contaminates it---was registered as a hypothesis
before the test and is false. The numerator alone also anti-correlates with dissipation at
high $\alpha$ ($\rho_S=-0.368$). The mechanism is identified ($\R$'s within-level variation
becomes denominator-driven, $\rho_S=0.955$) but the mechanism does not repair the
decoupling.
\item[\textbf{(C3)}] \textbf{A spectral frontier, and criticality escape.} A critical
precision $\lambda_c(\alpha)$ exists only above $\alpha\approx0.55$ and descends
monotonically thereafter; potential games \emph{escape} criticality at high $\lambda$
because equilibrium concentration suppresses the choice covariance faster than $\lambda$
amplifies it.
\item[\textbf{(C4)}] \textbf{Observability.} The dissipation axis is recoverable from
observed trajectories alone---a $k$-block KLD estimator tracks the exact meter at rank
correlation $1.0$, and a debiased finite-time thermodynamic-uncertainty
bound~\cite{barato2015} certifies a lower bound at median tightness $0.85$---and both axes
are calibrated at machine precision against systems whose payoffs are known by construction.
\item[\textbf{(C5)}] \textbf{Software and reproducibility.} \strataq~\cite{strataq}, an
open JAX library, is the first published implementation of the Candogan decomposition, of
$\chieq$ for logit QRE, and of agent-aware entropy production. Every figure and number in
this paper regenerates from committed artifacts and fixed seeds.
\end{itemize}

To the best of the author's knowledge this is the first statement that the local and global
irreversibility measures of a strategic system are structurally inequivalent. No claim is
made that either meter supersedes the other, and no claim is made about the sign of the
residual correlation: Section~\ref{sec:results} shows that sign to be $\lambda$- and
$m$-dependent, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} treats it as an open question rather than a
result.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} Registered before the runs reported here, with the test and the
level fixed in advance (criteria files are committed and their commits verified landed;
see Appendix~\ref{app:prov}).
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{H1}] Within-$\alpha$-level $\rho_S(\EPR,\R)$ declines as $\alpha\to1$.
\emph{Test:} Spearman within each of ten $\alpha$ levels, $100$ games per level; monotone
decline over the top three levels. \emph{Primary.}
\item[\textbf{H2a}] The decline is a ratio artefact: $\|\chi-\chi^{\top}\|_F$ alone retains
co-movement with $\EPR$ at high $\alpha$ ($\rho_S>0.5$).
\item[\textbf{H2b}] At high $\alpha$, $\R$'s within-level variation is denominator-driven:
$\rho_S(\R,1/\|\chi+\chi^{\top}\|_F)>0.8$.
\item[\textbf{H3}] The collapse is universal in $\lambda\in\{0.8,1.2,2.0\}$ and
$m\in\{3,4\}$; \textbf{H3b}: its residual sign is $\lambda$-invariant.
\item[\textbf{H4}] Both coordinates are estimable on real systems without payoff knowledge.
\item[\textbf{H5}] \emph{Kill-shot, finite size.} The collapse is an artefact of $m=3$ and
recovers as the action set grows. \emph{Test:} within-level $\rho_S$ over
$m\in\{3,4,5,6\}$, $200$ games per $(m,\alpha)$ cell, $N=2$, $\lambda=1.2$, percentile
bootstrap ($2000$ resamples) on every cell. Writing
$\mathrm{gap}(m)=\rho_S(m,0.05)-\rho_S(m,0.95)$, the claim \emph{dies} iff all three of
(a) $\rho_S(m,0.95)$ is monotone non-decreasing in $m$ to within $0.05$, (b)
$\mathrm{gap}(6)/\mathrm{gap}(3)<0.50$, and (c) the terminal intervals at $m=3$ and $m=6$
are disjoint; it \emph{survives} iff all three of $\mathrm{gap}(m)/\mathrm{gap}(3)\ge0.50$,
$\rho_S(m,0.95)\le0.35$, and the upper interval endpoint below $0.35$, at every $m$.
Anything else is \textbf{indeterminate}---the branches are deliberately not exhaustive, so
the run cannot pass by default. Precondition: $\rho_S(m,0.05)\ge0.55$ at every $m$.
\emph{Primary.}
\item[\textbf{H5b}] \emph{Onset.} The $\alpha$ at which the collapse begins (smallest
swept level with $\rho_S<0.5$) does not drift upward with $m$ by more than $0.10$, one grid
step.
\item[\textbf{H6}] \emph{Kill-shot, solver.} The collapse is an artefact of the damped
fixed-point solver's finite-precision $\sigma^*$. \emph{Test:} recompute the
$\alpha\in\{0.85,0.95\}$ cells at $m=3$ on the identical seed family with magnetic mirror
descent (a structurally different algorithm) and with the damped solver at a $100\times$
tighter tolerance; the collapse is numerical if $|\Delta\rho_S|\ge0.05$ in any of the four
comparisons. $\EPR$ is built from the generator and never touches the solver, so the solver
can enter this correlation only through $\R$.
\item[\textbf{H7}] \emph{Kill-shot, player count.} The high-$\alpha$ decorrelation is an
$N=2$ artefact and coupling recovers at $N>2$. \emph{Test:} within-level $\rho_S$ at
$N\in\{2,3,4\}$, $m=3$, $400$ games per cell, with a paired arm matching the median
normalised precision across $N$ to within $10\%$; the claim \emph{dies} iff
$\rho_S(\alpha{=}0.95)>0.35$ with its lower interval endpoint also above $0.35$ at some
$N>2$, and \emph{holds} iff the upper interval endpoint is below $0.35$ at every $N$.
\textbf{Precondition:} $\rho_S(\alpha{=}0.05)\ge0.55$ at every $N$---without a baseline
coupling there is no collapse to measure. \textbf{H7b}: the onset does not drift upward in
$N$ by more than $0.10$. \emph{Primary.}
\item[\textbf{H8}] \emph{Kill-shot, ratio artefact across $m$.} H2a's refutation is an
$m=3$ artefact. \emph{Test:} $\rho_S(\EPR,\|\chi-\chi^{\top}\|_F)$ at $\alpha=0.95$ for
$m\in\{3,4,5,6\}$ at $N=2$, with the ratio computed on the identical games and their
paired difference reported.
\end{itemize}

\noindent H5 and H6 were registered in a configuration file committed \emph{before} the
experiment implementing them existed. H5 was registered \emph{twice}: the criterion stated
above is the second registration, and an earlier one---adjudicating the raw recovery
$\rho_S(6,0.95)-\rho_S(3,0.95)$ against bars of $0.40$ and $0.20$---was live when the first
two runs of this sweep were launched. The substitution, the disagreement between the two on
the same data, and the verdict we report as a result are set out in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness} and among the limitations in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. The
outcome---\textbf{indeterminate} on H5 with the claim resting on the ceiling condition
instead, a solver-noise diagnostic rather than a survived kill-shot on H6, and a confound
found along the way---is carried in the scoreboard (Table~\ref{tab:scoreboard}) with the same
wording as the hypotheses that failed.
