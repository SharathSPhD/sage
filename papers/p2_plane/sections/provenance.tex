\noindent\textbf{Kill-shot registration, and the substitution inside it.} The criteria for
H5, H5b, H6 and the D1 payoff-scale control were written into \texttt{plane\_robustness}'s
configuration and committed \emph{before} the experiment file implementing them existed;
neither that file nor its predecessor was amended after a result existed, and the branch that
fires when the design cannot discriminate (\emph{indeterminate}) was registered alongside the
pass and fail branches so the run could not be read as confirmation by default. \emph{It is
not, however, true that this unit had a single criterion throughout.} H5 was first registered
in \texttt{plane\_finite\_size.yaml} (committed 23:02:25 on 2026-08-12), an experiment
implementing it was executed at 23:04:49 and again at 23:17:47, and
\texttt{plane\_robustness.yaml} was committed at 23:26:36 with a different deciding statistic
for the same kill-shot. The superseded configuration, its experiment and its resolved
configuration are committed alongside the artifacts below, so the ordering of registration
and execution is checkable from the repository rather than asserted here; the verdict this
paper reports for H5 is the earlier registration's. The $m=3$ cells of that sweep reconstruct the
\texttt{chain\_comovement} seed stream exactly, so the first $100$ games of each are the
same games: the measured deviation from the published values at $\alpha\in\{0.85,0.95\}$ is
$0.000$.

\noindent\textbf{Corrections carried in the record.} Six claims made earlier in this
programme were withdrawn and the withdrawals are part of the evidence: a reading of the
finite-size sweep as a narrow \emph{survival}, corrected to \emph{indeterminate} once the
earlier registration of the same criterion was accounted for; a statement that the registered
payoff-scale control removes the $m$-trend entirely, corrected to \emph{largely, not wholly};
a statement that the sign reversal ``does not survive $m$'', corrected by the paired
precision-matched control of Table~\ref{tab:c1b}, which finds $-0.206$ $[-0.300,-0.105]$ at
$m=6$ and attributes roughly $40\%$ of the apparent decay to temperature rather than
dimension; an early statement that $\R$ is $\lambda$-free (only the zero test is); an
intermediate ``certified null'' verdict on a five-minute electricity series that was
retracted when the null class was shown not to bracket the data; and a transcription of the
stratified sweep's precision as $\lambda=1.5$, which the resolved configuration of
\texttt{chain\_comovement} shows to be $\lambda=1.2$ (Table~\ref{tab:collapse} carries the
corrected value, and the robustness sweep was run at $1.2$ for that reason). All six are
reflected in the text above.

\noindent\textbf{Gate status, stated because a reader is entitled to it.} Neither of the two
units behind Sections~\ref{sec:robustness} and~\ref{sec:nplayers} carries an adversarial
signoff. The finite-size unit's review returned \emph{withheld} on the criterion
substitution and the missing interval; the corrections above were applied and committed, but
the unit has not been re-reviewed. Its registered prior-art re-audit \emph{has} since been
run, first-hand rather than inherited, and its outcome---that the structural inequivalence of
a local response object and a global dissipation object is prior art in stochastic
thermodynamics~\cite{baiesi2009} and is false in the Langevin class~\cite{haradasasa2005}---is
what narrows the novelty claim as stated in Sections~\ref{sec:intro}
and~\ref{sec:bg:priorart}. The $N$-scaling unit has had no adversarial review at all. Both now have gate files, and both
gates are \textbf{red} on exactly those grounds rather than absent from the board.
