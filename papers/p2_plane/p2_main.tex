\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Logit revision dynamics on a finite game define a Markov jump process whose stationary
state is a genuine thermodynamic object: a Gibbs equilibrium exactly when the game is
potential, and a dissipative non-equilibrium steady state otherwise. Two observables are
routinely treated as interchangeable readings of ``how far from equilibrium'' such a system
sits---the Onsager asymmetry $\R$ of the equilibrium response matrix
$\chieq=(I-SB)^{-1}S$, and the Schnakenberg entropy production $\EPR$ of the revision
chain. Both vanish if and only if the normalised game is potential, and unconditionally
they co-move almost perfectly (Spearman $\rho_S=0.993$, $n=1000$). \emph{They are not the
same coordinate.} Conditioning on the harmonic fraction $\alpha$ of the Candogan flow
decomposition, their co-movement \emph{collapses}: against that same $0.993$ marginal, the
within-level association falls from $\rho_S\approx+0.72$ at $\alpha=0.05$ to a value whose
upper $95\%$ interval endpoint is below $0.35$ at every action count tested
$m\in\{3,4,5,6\}$ (worst $+0.156$), with the low-$\alpha$ and high-$\alpha$ intervals
disjoint at every $m$, at $N=2$. Independence requires decorrelation, not anti-correlation,
and decorrelation is what is established. The residual is additionally negative
($\rho_S=-0.35$ $[-0.47,-0.23]$ at $m=3$, $\lambda=1.2$, $N=2$), and that sign reversal is
a scoped fact rather than a general one: it moves with $\lambda$, and at \emph{fixed}
Frobenius payoff scale it weakens with $m$ (to $-0.112$ $[-0.214,-0.016]$ at $m=6$,
$n=400$), while under a \emph{paired control that matches normalised precision by
construction} it survives to $m=6$ ($-0.206$ $[-0.300,-0.105]$). It is therefore quoted only
with its $(m,\lambda,N)$ attached, and the claim is rested on the collapse, not on it. The obvious repair---that the
collapse is an artefact of $\R$ being a norm ratio---was pre-registered and is refuted by
its own test: the numerator $\|\chi-\chi^{\top}\|_F$ alone reaches $\rho_S=-0.368$, so no
renormalisation of the response matrix recovers the dissipation ordering. This paper is
not a claim that either meter is wrong. It is a claim about \emph{dimension}: distance from
equilibrium is not a scalar for strategic systems, and the $(\R,\EPR)$ plane---not either
axis---is the space in which an observed system should be classified. Both pre-registered
kill-shots on that claim have now been run ($5200$ games, $6000$ solves).
\emph{Finite size: the outcome is \textbf{indeterminate}, and we report why rather than
around it.} Two registrations of this criterion exist. The earlier one adjudicates the raw
recovery $\rho_S(6,0.95)-\rho_S(3,0.95)$ against bars of $0.40$ (refutation) and $0.20$
(survival); the later one, committed after two runs had already been launched under the
earlier, adjudicates the gap ratio $\mathrm{gap}(6)/\mathrm{gap}(3)$ against $0.50$. On the
same data the recovery is $+0.364$---resolving neither branch---while the gap ratio is
$0.585$, above its bar; the first registration therefore returns \textbf{indeterminate}
where the second returns survival, and the deciding gap ratio's bootstrap interval crosses
its own bar. We report the earlier verdict, disclose the substitution, and rest the claim
elsewhere. \emph{What the claim rests on is the ceiling criterion, and it is not close:} the
high-$\alpha$ association never recovers at any tested action count---the worst upper
interval endpoint is $+0.156$ against a $0.35$ ceiling, and the $\alpha=0.05$ and
$\alpha=0.95$ intervals are disjoint at every $m$. That, not a gap ratio, is what
two-coordinate independence requires. \emph{Solver:} on identical seed families a
structurally different algorithm and a $100\times$ tighter tolerance perturb $\R$ by
$\sim\!10^{-12}$ and leave the rank ordering bit-identical, so the collapse is not
numerical---reported as the solver-noise diagnostic it is, since a rank statistic could not
have registered a perturbation that small. A registered control on the payoff scale shows
the apparent finite-size decay is largely, though not wholly, a normalised-precision effect
of the game generator rather than a dimensional one. \emph{$N$-scaling has since been run, and it narrows the evidence rather than the claim.}
At three and four players ($16{,}000$ solves, $400$ games per cell, with a paired arm that
matches normalised precision to within $3.1\%$) the ceiling holds with the widest margin
this programme has recorded---worst upper endpoint $+0.163$ against $0.35$---which is
consistent with two independent coordinates. But the registered \emph{precondition} fails:
low-$\alpha$ coupling, the thing the whole stratified design measures a departure from,
falls $+0.847\to+0.378\to+0.172$ across $N=2,3,4$ at matched precision, so at $N\ge3$
there is no baseline to collapse from and the kill-shot returns \textbf{indeterminate}. We
decline to read absence-of-coupling-everywhere as confirmation. \textbf{The
$\alpha$-stratified collapse is a two-player instrument}, and every statement in this paper
that rests on it carries $N=2$. We state the remaining scope honestly: the collapse is
universal across every precision $\lambda$ and action count $m$ tested at $N=2$, but the
\emph{sign} of the residual is $\lambda$- and $m$-dependent, so the second dimension exists
everywhere it can be measured while its tilt is itself a system property. We calibrate both coordinates
at machine precision on systems with payoffs known by construction (Sioux Falls road
network, $\R=5.6\times10^{-17}$; Colonel Blotto, $\alpha=0.69$, $\R=0.12$), show the
dissipation axis is recoverable from observed trajectories alone (rank correlation $1.0$
against the exact meter; a certified finite-time thermodynamic-uncertainty lower bound at
median tightness $0.85$), and place two real systems in the plane---a retail scanner panel
at $\R=0.0011$ $[5\times10^{-5},5\times10^{-3}]$ and a wholesale electricity market at
$\approx1.07$ nats/day---which fall in \emph{different} quadrants. One quadrant of the four
is not yet occupied by any measured system, and locating one there is the decisive test we
name in advance. All software is open (\strataq, Apache-2.0); every quantitative statement
regenerates from fixed seeds, and Appendix~\ref{app:prov} maps each number to its artifact.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantal response equilibrium; non-equilibrium steady state;
entropy production; Onsager reciprocity; potential games; Hodge decomposition; bounded
rationality
